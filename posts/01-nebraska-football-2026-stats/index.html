This is a story about Nebraska Football. Today, we are going to explore the losses the team (and the poor fans) have suffered and breaking down the reasons behind them.

If you’re a Husker fan, or have paid any attention to college football in the last 10 or so years, you’ll know that Nebraska has been one of the more pitiful programs in the country as of recently. After the loss to Illinois on Nov. 6, 2026, a 62-79 record since the firing of Bo Pelini, including a nine-year bowl drought that ended in 2024, STILL zero ranked wins, an insurmountable number of one-score losses, three head coaches (plus a controversial interim), and the list goes on and on.

If you were to ask someone about the woes from the 2010’s and early 2020’s, the easiest answer to this question would be the turnovers. The Blackshirts’ odd inability to force turnovers along with some embarrassing fumbles and interception-prone quarterbacks paired together to earn the Huskers four seasons since 2015 with a double-digit negative turnover margin. The Big Red didn’t right that ship until 2025 when they accomplished a positive turnover margin for the first time since 2009.

However, Matt Rhule came into this program in 2023 as a “program-builder” and the Nebraska faithful put high expectations on him right away. The turnovers were cleaned up, but the success on the field still hadn’t shown up as much as desired. Once all said and done, Rhule’s teams the last few years have finished the season with records hovering around the .500 mark.

That begs the question: What is the issue now?

Code 
winlosslogs <- logs24 |>
  mutate(
    wins = case_when(
      grepl("W", Result) ~ 1,
      grepl("L", Result) ~ 0
    )
  )

yardsper <- winlosslogs |>
  group_by(Team, Conference) |>
  summarise(
    total_plays = sum(OffensivePlays),
    total_yards = sum(OffensiveYards),
    defensive_plays = sum(DefPlays),
    defensive_yards = sum(DefYards),
    total_wins = sum(wins)
  ) |>
  mutate(
    offensive_ypp = total_yards / total_plays,
    defensive_ypp = defensive_yards / defensive_plays
  )

nu <- yardsper |>
  filter(Team == "Nebraska")

ggplot() +
  geom_point(
    data = yardsper,
    aes(x = offensive_ypp, y = defensive_ypp, size = total_wins),
    alpha = .3
  ) +
  scale_size(range = c(3, 8), name = "Wins") +
  geom_point(
    data = nu,
    aes(
      x = offensive_ypp,
      y = defensive_ypp,
      size = total_wins
    ),
    color = "red"
  )+
  geom_vline(xintercept = 5.763149) +
  geom_hline(yintercept= 5.601157)

Ahh, the classic, a good defense and subpar offense.

But why is that the case? In non-conference play in 2024, the Big Red averaged 34 points a game including a 28-10 beatdown of a Colorado team that finished 9-4, where the Blackshirts forced Heisman winner Shedeur Sanders to his worst outing of the regular season.

This is just the season overall, though. How do the results shape out as the season progresses?

Hmmm. We may have found Nebraska’s kryptonite. Looking at the Big Ten we see the consistently good offensive teams - Oregon, Penn State, USC, and the consistently bad teams - Northwestern, Rutgers, and Maryland. However, we look at the Huskers offense and we see it’s quite consistently… inconsistent.

Let’s compare this to the 2026 season and see how the offense plays out.

So, overall, what is the issue?

The Big Ten is a tough, gritty conference that heavily relies on defense if you want any chance of winning on the field. The Huskers have done their job on that end of the field so far this season - allowing their opponents to score just 16.5 points a game. But you need an offense as well.

The Big Ten is often thought of as a “race to 21 points,” and that much has been clear this season. When NU scores more than 21 points, they win. When they don’t, they lose. You can rein as much hell on offense and drive down the field as much as you want, but if you can’t finish the drives with a score, your chances of winning are as bleak as they can possibly be.

Time is ticking on Matt Rhule’s tenure as head coach, and after firing Offensive Coordinator Dana Holgorsen following the embarrassing loss to Illinois, Rhule needs to make a splash to keep his career moving and keep the Husker faithful believing in him.
